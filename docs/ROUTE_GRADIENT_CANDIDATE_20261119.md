# Candidate: give hard layer choices a counterfactual gradient

Not admitted or launched. Decide after the causal-cost report/replay/handoff. Existing runs and controls remain immutable.

The frozen single-edit oracle has limited headroom. Posthoc inspection on the same128 diagnostic validation examples gives at most0.00326/0.00292 nats for a fixed LF depth. A larger frozen head cannot manufacture better operators. The alternative hypothesis is that joint training has supplied weak credit to hard choices: the current argmax is nondifferentiable, and ordinary router gradients arrive through the selected probability gate, not through the change of expert identity. This is a code-derived hypothesis, not an established diagnosis.

Straight-through estimators are heuristic surrogates for gradients through hard decisions; they are not automatically unbiased RL estimators. See [Bengio, Léonard and Courville, 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3432). Sparse routing also introduces optimization and stability issues discussed by [Fedus, Zoph and Shazeer, 2022](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v23/21-0998.html). Neither source establishes that this proposed whole-block routing variant will win.

Candidate experiment: retain exactly the parent's hard forward computation, but add a zero-valued correction carrying derivatives of alternative block outputs to the router. At a decision, let `p` be router probabilities, `e` the selected expert, and `delta[k]` the block residual. The existing forward is `p[e] * delta[e]`. A candidate surrogate adds `sum_k (p[k] - stopgrad(p[k])) * stopgrad(p[k] * delta[k])`. Its forward value is exactly zero; only the surrogate route derivative is added. The existing selected-gate gradient is retained. This is deliberately biased and distinct from differentiating a soft mixture. Alternative block residuals are detached in the correction so unselected experts do not acquire unintended direct gradients.
